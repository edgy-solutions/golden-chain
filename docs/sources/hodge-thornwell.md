# Source report: Hodge and Thornwell on the validity of Romish baptism (1845–1852)

Phase 1, step 3 (dispatch 2026-11-08 §7: validity position 1, "Hodge against Thornwell"). Research done 2026-11-08. No text is stored in this repo. Locators were proposed by a text-only subagent from the scans' OCR; the build agent checked those marked [V] on the page images.

## The controversy

- The 1845 Old School General Assembly decided, 169 to 8 with 6 non liquet, that Romish baptism is invalid. [R: BRPR p. 444]
- The *Biblical Repertory and Princeton Review* for July 1845 dissented, in an unsigned article. [V p. 469]
- Thornwell answered, first as articles signed "Henley" (1846), later collected in the *Southern Presbyterian Review* (1851–52), then in his *Collected Writings*. [R: editor's note, CW3 p. 279]

## Finding for the architect: (1)'s wording omits the administrator

- The dispatch words (1) as "subject and formula suffice".
- The BRPR article's grounds at p. 469 include the administrator: a publicly called presbyter who is a member of the visible church. WCF 28.2 likewise requires "a minister of the gospel, lawfully called thereunto".
- So (1) as its sources hold it is "matter, formula and a lawfully called minister suffice, whatever the administrator's church". The difference from (2) is mode and subject; the difference from (3) and (4) is the administering church, not the presence of an administrator.
- Hodge's ST §6 holds an unordained administrator irregular but not invalid, which is weaker than the 1845 article. The wording of (1) is the architect's call.
