## Edition and public domain

- **Edition:** Philip Schaff, *The Creeds of Christendom*, vol. 3, *The Evangelical Protestant Creeds*. New York: Harper, 1878 printing (first edition 1877). Schaff died in 1893. Public domain. [R]
- **Scan:** archive.org `creedsofchristen187803scha` (Princeton Theological Seminary Library copy, full view, 914 images). [R] An 1881 printing under the title *Bibliotheca symbolica ecclesiae universalis* (`bibliothecasymbo03scha`) was not compared page by page. [U]
- **Leaf map:** archive.org leaf `n` = printed page + 15 near both places used. Checked at p. 331 (n346), p. 424 (n439) and p. 427 (n442). The subagent's offsets were off by 1 for the Heidelberg pages and by 3 for the Belgic pages; use only the checked map. [V]
- **Cite as:** Question (Heidelberg) or Article (Belgic), with the printed page of the 1878 printing.

### Heidelberg in Schaff

- German and English in parallel columns, numbered by Question. [V p. 331]
- The English is the 1863 Tercentenary translation (German Reformed Church in the US), printed by permission. It is PD by date. [R]
- **No Scripture proofs are printed** under Q.65–74. Schaff says the proofs are in his separate German Tercentenary edition. [R]
- **For proofs:** Harbaugh, *The Heidelberg Catechism with proof-texts* (Reading, Pa.: D. Miller, 1889), archive.org `heidelbergcatech00harb`, gives proofs in a "How do you prove this?" form; for Q.74, Gen 17:11–12 and Col 2:11–12. Not checked on the image. [R]

### Belgic in Schaff

- French ("Revised 1619") and English in parallel columns, numbered by Article in roman numerals with Latin headings. [V p. 424] Schaff also prints a Latin text, which he says differs considerably. [R]
- The English is the text authorised by the Reformed (Dutch) Church in America, made from the Latin. So it belongs to the Dort-revised family, not to the 1561 French directly. Numbering is the 37-article Dort numbering. [R]
- **Proofs are footnoted** under each Article, keyed to the French column. [V pp. 424, 427]

**What the texts deny.** Heidelberg Q.72 (p. 330) says the outward washing does not itself wash away sin; only Christ's blood and the Holy Spirit do. Q.73 (pp. 330–331) calls baptism a divine pledge and token by which God assures us. Belgic Art. XXXIV (pp. 425–426) says the cleansing is not effected by the external water. [R, except Q.73's answer on p. 331: V] So both texts hold (a) with the Spirit as agent and the sacrament as sign, seal and means. This fits the excludes edge's note, that (a) as Reformed theology holds it includes representation (RULING_report_11).

**Heidelberg proofs.** The Argument for 23b-yes from Q.74 has no proof text in Schaff. Its Passages would have to come from Harbaugh 1889 (Gen 17:11–12; Col 2:11–12), from Belgic XXXIV's footnotes, or from WCF 28.4's own proofs. This is recorded as an architect finding.
