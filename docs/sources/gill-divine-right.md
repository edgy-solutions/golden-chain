## Proposed locators

| Position | Locator | What it carries | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| 23b-no | Ch. IV, pp. 44–62 | Gill denies that the Gen 17 covenant is the pure covenant of grace (Enquiry 1, pp. 44–47). He denies that circumcision sealed it to the natural seed; Rom 4:11 sealed it to Abraham personally (Enquiry 3, pp. 56–58). Baptism did not succeed circumcision (Enquiry 4, pp. 58–62). Scripture cited: Gen 17; Acts 7:8; Gal 5:2–4; Rom 4:11–17; Rom 9:6–7. | [R] |
| repetition (secondary) | Ch. II, pp. 5–6 | The charge is that one who adopts believers' baptism renounces his baptism. Gill replies that infant baptism must first be proved to be baptism, and that it has no warrant. This is a burden-of-proof argument, weaker than Booth's. | [R] |

**Limits on what may be cited.**

- **23b.** Gill grants a "mixed" covenant with a spiritual part. Cite him for 23b-no as worded in the dispatch, and not for any stronger form.
- **Repetition.** Booth is the primary source and Gill the secondary ([booth-paedobaptism-examined.md](booth-paedobaptism-examined.md)).

**Relevant to validity (3)/(4), not proposed as a locator.** Gill's replies to the 2nd–5th consequences (Ch. II, pp. 9–13) concern administrators and succession. He takes John the Baptist as the first administrator, himself unbaptized. [R; check before use]

## Acts 19:5 (dispatch §6 CAUTION)

- In the Exposition, Gill reads Acts 19:4–5 as Paul's own words describing how John's hearers were baptized. He says they are "not the words of Luke the Evangelist". He takes the historian's narration to resume at v. 6, because the particles of vv. 4 and 5 answer each other. So he denies a rebaptism. [V: Exposition text]
- The dispatch says "Gill read v. 5 as Luke's report of John's baptism". It has the conclusion right (no rebaptism) and the speaker wrong (Paul, not Luke). This is recorded as an architect finding.
- In the *Divine Right* (about p. 20), Acts 19:1–7 is listed among the adult baptisms without exegesis. [R]
