## Edition and public domain

- **Booth died in 1806.** Every edition cited here is public domain. [R]
- **Edition used for locators:** the 1829 third edition, in 3 vols., London. Princeton copies are on archive.org:

- **Leaf map (vol. 2):** archive.org leaf `n` = printed page + 9 near p. 280. [V]
- **Cite as:** 1829, vol., Part, printed page, and the note mark where there is one.

## Proposed locators for the repetition position

Position (dispatch §6): "Baptism is administered once. The sprinkling of an infant is not baptism, so the baptism of a professing believer who was sprinkled in infancy is his first baptism, not a repetition."

| Locator | What it carries | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| 1829 vol. 2, Part II "General Remarks", p. 280, note ‡, continued on p. 281 | Opponents should not call Baptists Anabaptists. The note gives the principle: a ceremony essentially different from what Christ appointed is not baptism, so one who received it may afterward be baptized without any charge of Anabaptism. It cites Wall, Buddeus, Venema, Bingham, Deylingius, Hoornbeek and Gerhard. | [V] |
| 1829 vol. 2, Part III, ch. II, pp. 398–399 (the *Defence*) | Baptists immerse those sprinkled in infancy and do not count it Anabaptism. | [R] |

**What is cited.** No Scripture is cited at either place. Booth argues from essential difference, as to mode and subject together. He does not argue from a text. So the passage for the Position's Argument is Eph 4:5 ("one baptism", per §6). Booth is the source division for the clause "infant sprinkling is not baptism".

**Also found (not proposed for phase 1).** Booth's *An Apology for the Baptists* (1778), Sect. II, states the claim more directly. That work is about communion, and the 1778 scan's paging was inferred from noisy headers. [R]
