- **In short:**
  - Every source answers J-a. The confessions, Owen, Witsius and Belgic say faith is an instrument or the means. Gill denies that faith is "even the instrumental cause": it is the effect and evidence of a justification already passed.
  - J-b parts the sources. WCF 11.4 and LBC 11.4 put a decree from eternity and justification when the Spirit applies Christ. Gill calls that eternal decree itself the justification (II.5, p. 207). Witsius counts several "articles", none from eternity.
  - J-c: the LC calls faith "the condition". Owen allows a condition in one sense. Witsius and Gill deny it.
  - **The lock missed twice.** Belgic answers J-b (Art. XXIII). Witsius answers J-c (III.8 §§XLIX-LII).
  - Six Positions are proposed, each worded only from the sources.

### Witsius, *Economy*, III.8 (1763)

| | |
|---|---|
| **J-a** | "The MEAN, by which we receive the righteousness of Christ, and justification depending thereon, is FAITH, and that ONLY." (§XLVII, p. 547). And: "Dutch confession speaks far more accurately; namely, that faith is here instead of an instrument, whereby we are joined together with Christ in a partnership or communion of all his benefits." (§LI, p. 551). And: "The genuine opinion of the reformed is this: that faith justifies, as it is the band of our strictest union with Christ, by which all things that are Christ's become also ours" (§LVI, p. 555). |
| **J-b** | "justification, if we take in whatever can be comprized under that name, consists of various articles, or periods" (§LVII, p. 556). And: "yet that right, purchased by Christ, is not applied to him, till he is regenerated, and united to Christ by faith." ... "But immediately, on his receiving Christ by faith, God declares in the court of heaven, that he is no longer under wrath, but under grace; tho' perhaps the justified person may yet be ignorant of it." (§LIX, p. 557). |
| **J-c** | "Nor do I think it an accurate way of speaking, that faith is the condition, which the Gospel requireth of us, in order to be accounted righteous and without guilt before God. The condition of justification, properly speaking, is perfect obedience only: this the law requires: nor does the Gospel substitute any other" (§LII, p. 552). And: "Tho' some of the reformed have said, that faith is a condition sine qua non, without which we cannot be justified: yet they were far from being of opinion, that faith is a condition properly so called, on performing which, man should, according to the gracious covenant of God, have a right to justification as to a reward." (§LII, p. 552). |

**Notes on Witsius:**
- His articles are two "general" ones and five particular ones:
  - General: one "commenced immediately upon the fall" (§LVII, p. 556). The other came when God raised Christ from the dead and "gave him a discharge, in testimony that the payment was made" (§LVIII, p. 557).
  - Particular: on receiving Christ by faith, in the court of heaven (§LIX). In the conscience (§LX, p. 558: "intimated and insinuated to the conscience by the Holy-Spirit"). In familiar converse with God (§LXI). Immediately after death (§LXII, p. 559). At the last day (§LXIII, p. 559).
- None of his articles is from eternity. In pp. 522-572, "from eternity" is used only of the kingdom (§LXVI, p. 560).
- The 1763 footnotes on pp. 555-556 are the translator's, not Witsius's. They are not used as his witness.
