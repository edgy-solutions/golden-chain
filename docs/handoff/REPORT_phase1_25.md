## The five questions, from Gill's words

Each answer is a quotation of at most three sentences, or "does not say in the sections read".

### Q1. Who may administer baptism: any believer, or only a minister of the word?

**A minister of the word.**
- III.1, p. 898 / PDF 951: "hence says Christ, to encourage his ministers to preach his gospel, and to baptize in his name; Lo, I am with you always, in the ministry of the word, and in the administration of baptism, even unto the end of the world, Matt. xxviii. 19, 20."
- II.3, p. 863 / PDF 916: "yet it is pretty plain, that those who have a commission to teach, have also a commission to baptize, and to attend to whatsoever Christ has commanded;"
- DR 1749, p. 10 / n13: "though upon the Principle of Adult Baptism, as necessary to the Communion of Churches, it follows, that no unbaptized Person is regularly called to the Preaching of the Word, and Administration of Ordinances, or can be a regular Communicant;"

### Q2. Who makes a man a minister or administrator?

**Ordinarily, a church's call, which with his acceptance is his ordination. In an extraordinary case, a call from God is enough.**
- II.3, p. 866 / PDF 919: "There must be a call to the ministry of the word, both inward and outward, previous to this office;" and "The outward call is not immediately by Christ, … but mediately by the church;"
- II.3, p. 867 / PDF 920: "The election and call of them, with their acceptance, is ordination."
- II.3, p. 875 / PDF 928: "the administration of an ordinance is an act of office-power, which one church cannot give to another". (p. 874 applies this to a pastor giving the Supper in another church.)
- DR 1749, p. 15 / n18 (the extraordinary case, restoring baptism): "it was enough for any Person sensible of the Corruption, to attempt a Reformation, and to administer it in the right Way, who was satisfied of his Call from God, to preach the Gospel, and administer Ordinances, according to the Word."

### Q3. What does "not a church ordinance" mean?

**That baptism is given outside the church, before membership and in order to it, unlike the Supper. It does not mean no church is needed.**
- III.1, p. 896 / PDF 949: "When I say it is not a church-ordinance, I mean it is not an ordinance administered in the church, but out of it, and in order to admission into it, and communion with it; it is preparatory to it, and a qualification for it, it does not make a person a member of a church, or admit him into a visible church;"
- III.1, p. 896: "a church has nothing to do with the baptism of any, but to be satisfied they are baptized before they are admitted into communion with it. Admission to baptism lies solely in the breast of the administrator…"
- II.1, p. 858 / PDF 911: "nor baptism, though a pre-requisite to church-fellowship, does not make a man a member of a church, as it did not the eunuch;" (and p. 856: "they must be baptized before they become members").
- The Supper, III.1, p. 915 / PDF 968: "none but such who have submitted to the former, ought to be admitted to the latter."

### Q4. May a church refuse one baptized by a given administrator, or require him to be baptized?

**Does not say in the sections read.** No passage read takes this case. The nearest:
- The church's general option, II.1, p. 857 / PDF 910: "a man may propose himself to be a member of a church, but it is at the option of the church whether they will receive him;"
- What makes a baptism, Reply 1765, p. 4 / n7: "The Essentials to the right Administration of Baptism amongst other Things are, that it be performed by Immersion, without which it can't be Baptism; and that it be administred upon a Profession of Faith; neither of which are to be found in Infant-Sprinkling."
- The administrator not essential: DR 1749, p. 14 (under Q5).
- Reply 1765, p. 13 / n16, on the first English Baptists, whether they sent abroad or used an unbaptized administrator: "they are able to justify their Baptism on as good a Foundation as the Reformers are able to justify theirs received from the Papists,"
- *Not Gill's words:* DR 1749, pp. 15–17, quotes Zanchy and, through him, Pope Nicholas that one so baptized "ought not to be baptized again". Gill adopts Zanchy's **reason** for the first English Baptists (p. 17 / n20: "The Reason which Zanchy gives, will, I think, hold good in the Case of the first Antipaedobaptists in England."). The quoted words are Zanchy's and are not cited as Gill's.

### Q5. Does Gill require a baptized administrator, or a succession? The first administrators?

**Ordinarily, a baptized administrator. It is not essential, and there is no succession. The first administrator, John, was unbaptized and had a commission from God.**
- DR 1749, p. 14 / n17: "It must be owned, that in ordinary Cases, he ought to be baptized himself, that baptizes another, or preaches the Word, or administers other Ordinances; but in an extraordinary Case as this, of beginning a Reformation, from a general Corruption, where such an Administrator cannot be had, it may be done;"
- DR 1749, p. 14 / n17, continuing: "nor is it essential to the Ordinance that there should be such an Administrator, or otherwise it could never have been introduced into the World at all at first; the first Administrator must be an unbaptized Person, as John the Baptist was."
- Reply 1765, p. 12 / n15: "but he has no where promised, that he will have a continued Succession of visible congregated Churches."
- III.1, p. 897 / PDF 950: "John had a mission and commission from God, he was a man sent of God, and sent to baptize, John i. 6, 33." (Sermon 1766, p. 8, says the same: "he did not administer it of his own mind and will, but had a mission and commission from God to do it;")
- *Context, not Gill's view:* DR 1749, p. 14, reports that others thought sending to the Waldenses "a needless Scruple, and looked too much like the Popish Notion of an uninterrupted Succession".

### One phrase to note

Gill uses "Gospel-Order" of a **church**, not of an administrator. Reply 1765, p. 13 / n16: "one small Church or Congregation gathered out of the World by the Grace of GOD, according to Gospel-Order, and whose Principles and Practices are agreeable to the Word of GOD, is to be preferred before all the national Churches in the World."

## The BSB 1984 foreword, conclusion 1(a)

*Amended after the ruling of 2026-11-22 ([RULING_bsb_foreword_status_2026-11-22.md](RULING_bsb_foreword_status_2026-11-22.md)), which permits this quotation in place of the paraphrase first given here.*

Foreword p. xiii (PDF 14), "IN CONCLUSION", 1. The publishers say they "cannot agree completely with Dr. Gill's statements in three areas". The first:
> "a. in his Practical Divinity, book three, Chpt. 1, pp. 896-915, his introductory remarks about baptism are confusing. If that were all he had ever said relative to this subject, it would have been, and no doubt is, a stumbling block. But, for those who have trouble with this particular passage we suggest they reserve their final opinion about Dr. Gill's views of baptism until they have read his 12 other works on this subject, which, D.V., we intend to re-publish in the not too distant future."

- The works appeared as *Gospel Baptism* (BSB 2006). The foreword does not name the twelve.
- 1(b), for the record, is on Practical II.6 (pp. 886–895, discipline). II.6 was read and says nothing on the administrator.
- **For comparison with (3)**, the two propositions the ruling names for validity:
  - Proposition 3 (p. vii): "Scriptural baptism is absolutely necessary to church constitution, organization and existence, so much so, that where there is no Scriptural baptism there is no Scriptural church. No baptism, no church."
  - Proposition 7 (p. viii): "Only churches of New Testament origin and New Testament order can give Scriptural baptism. Therefore, any religious society that preaches a false gospel cannot give Scriptural baptism."
  - Proposition 7 puts the giving of baptism in **churches**. Gill puts admission to baptism "solely in the breast of the administrator" (III.1, p. 896) and makes the baptized administrator "not essential" (DR 1749, p. 14). That is the difference behind proposal (B).

## Proposal: (B)

**Proposed wording for a new Position under `locus/baptism-validity`, built from Gill's phrases:**

> "Immersion, upon a profession of faith, are the essentials of baptism. In ordinary cases the administrator ought to be baptized and called to preach the word and administer ordinances; but such an administrator is not essential to the ordinance, and admission to baptism lies in the administrator, not in the church."

- Phrases from: Reply p. 4 ("Essentials", "Immersion", "Profession of Faith"); DR p. 14 ("in ordinary Cases, he ought to be baptized himself", "not essential to the Ordinance"); DR p. 10 and II.3 p. 866 (called to preach and administer); III.1 p. 896 ("Admission to baptism lies solely in the breast of the administrator").
- "In the triune name" is left out. Gill's essentials passage does not list it, and this report does not add words he did not write here.

**Why not (A).**
- (3) makes the administrator, "a church of gospel order", one of the conditions of a valid baptism.
- Gill makes a baptized administrator not essential, and makes admission the administrator's act, not the church's.
- (3) agrees with Gill only in its last clause, "not by identity or succession" (Reply p. 12; DR p. 14).
- Gill's "Gospel-Order" describes a church (Reply p. 13), not the administrator.

**Why (B) rather than (C).**
- (C), "a minister called by a gospel church", is Gill's **ordinary rule** (II.3 pp. 863–867; DR p. 10).
- Gill does not make it a condition of validity. In the extraordinary case, a call from God suffices (DR p. 15), and an unbaptized administrator may begin (DR p. 14).
- So (C) as a validity Position would claim more than Gill says.

**An observation for the architect (not a decision).**
- On validity alone, (B) says what (2) "subject, mode and formula" says. The difference is (B)'s order clause, which governs regular practice, not whether a baptism is a baptism.
- So there is a second way to record Gill: a Gill Holding on (2), with the order clause in the Argument text, and no new Position.
- (B) is proposed because the dispatch's question is about the administrator, and (2) has nothing to say on it.
