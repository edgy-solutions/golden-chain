```
ARCHITECT DISPATCH — validity (3) and Gill on the administrator.
Research and propose only. Lock first. Mint nothing.

Chris's question: does Gill's "not a church ordinance" paragraph
(Practical III.1, opening) contradict "the administrator is a
church of gospel order", or does Gill tie the administrator to a
church by another route? Answer each from Gill's own words, a
quotation of at most three sentences with locator, or "does not
say in the sections read":
 Q1. Who may administer baptism: any believer, or only a minister
     of the word?
 Q2. Who makes a man a minister or administrator: a church's call
     and ordination, or something else?
 Q3. What does "not a church ordinance" mean in its context: that
     no church is needed, or that baptism is administered before
     and in order to membership, unlike the Supper?
 Q4. May a church refuse one baptized by a given administrator, or
     require him to be baptized? On what ground?
 Q5. Does Gill require a baptized administrator, or a succession of
     them? What does he say of the first administrators?
Sources: Practical III.1, the whole chapter and its head on the
administrator; Practical Book II on the gospel church, its officers
and the admission of members; Gill's separate baptism tracts (the
BSB foreword counts twelve; list them, with PD witness, and read
those that treat the administrator). Record that the BSB 1984
foreword, conclusion 1(a), calls these introductory remarks
"confusing" and defers to those tracts.
Propose one of: (A) Gill's view agrees with (3) in substance, with
the passages; (B) Gill's view is a distinct Position, worded from
his text; (C) a third Position, "a minister called by a gospel
church", if his words support it. Rows for sitting 2.
```
