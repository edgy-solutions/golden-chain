    - Belgic XXIX and Scots XVIII each name three: preaching, the sacraments, discipline.

  - **C-c (governs, speaks for).**
    - WCF: officers, and synods that determine "ministerially".
    - Belgic: a council of pastors, elders and deacons.

  - **C-d (who administers).**
    - Belgic XXX: ministers or pastors.
    - LBC 26.8: the officers, "for the peculiar Administration of Ordinances".
    - Gill: only an officer, and a pastor only in his own church. A private member may not be deputed, and deacons do not administer.

**Other Gill passages, for the architect:**

- **Officers.**
  - "The ordinary officers of the church are pastors and deacons, and these only;" (II.3, p. 863).
  - "...teachers design gifted brethren, called to minister the word, but not to office-power; and are only assistants to pastors in preaching, but not in the administration of the ordinances;" (II.3, p. 863). Compare LBC 26.11.
  - "Nor is their work and business to rule in the church; we read of ruling elders, but never of ruling deacons;" (II.5, p. 882).
- **Ordination.**
  - "The essence of ordination lies in the voluntary choice and call of the people, and in the voluntary acceptance of that call by the person chosen and called;" (II.3, p. 867).
  - "3. No instance can be given of hands being laid on any ordinary minister, pastor or elder, at his ordination;" (II.3, p. 868). Compare LBC 26.9.
- **Who speaks for the church.**
  - The pastor offers "public prayers put up to God by the minister, as the mouth of the church;" (II.6, p. 892).
  - The pastors govern "according to the laws and rules which Christ has given;" (II.4, p. 878).
  - "...every thing delivered by pastors of churches is not binding on churches; nor are they obliged to receive it, but as it accords with the word of God." (II.4, p. 879).
- **The keys.** They "have respect, not at all to discipline, but to doctrine." (II.6, p. 887). Compare WCF 30.2: "To these Officers, the Keys ... are committed:" (n59).
- **Synods and presbyteries.**
  - "...nor presbyterian; for we never read of a church of presbyters or elders, though of elders ordained in churches;" (II.1, p. 858).
  - Excommunication is not to be done "by the elders of a church separately; much less by the elders of other churches;" (II.6, p. 894).

## Joined: Gill on the administrator (report 25, Q1-Q5, with Book II)

Report 25's answers are cited, not re-read. Book II was read in full under this item. The table shows, for each question, what Book II adds.

| Q (report 25) | Report 25's answer | What Book II adds (this item) |
|---|---|---|
| Q1. Who may administer baptism? | A minister of the word (III.1 p. 898; II.3 p. 863; DR 1749 p. 10). | **Agrees.** The Supper is "an act of office-power" (p. 876). A private member may not be deputed to give it (p. 876). Deacons do not "administer ordinances, as baptism and the Lord's Supper" (p. 882). |
| Q2. Who makes a man a minister? | Ordinarily, a church's call, which with his acceptance is his ordination. In an extraordinary case, a call from God (II.3 pp. 866, 867, 875; DR 1749 p. 15). | **Agrees, and adds a limit.** A pastor may not give the Supper in a church of which he is not pastor (p. 874). A church "is a body corporate, in a spiritual sense; and its officers can only act as such within it, and within no other." (p. 874). Gill also says it "may justly create a scruple" to receive the ordinance "in a church where the administrator is not a pastor" (p. 875). |
| Q3. "Not a church ordinance" | Baptism is given outside the church and before membership (III.1 p. 896; II.1 p. 858). | **Agrees.** "Besides the ordinance of baptism, which is preparatory to church-communion," (II.6, p. 892). |
| Q4. May a church refuse one baptized by a given administrator? | Does not say in the sections read (II.1 p. 857: "at the option of the church"). | **Still does not say.** Members are received "by mutual consent" (p. 891), but no passage takes up who baptized the person. |
| Q5. A baptized administrator, or a succession? | Ordinarily a baptized administrator, but "not essential to the Ordinance". No succession (DR 1749 p. 14; Reply p. 12). | **Does not say in Book II.** Nothing in pp. 852-895 takes this up. |

**P3.8 held:** nothing in Book II contradicts report 25's Q1 or Q2.

**A note for the architect:** the Book II passages on the Supper (pp. 874-876) are about the Supper, not baptism. Gill himself sets the two apart (Q3). So these passages bear on Q1 only by way of "office-power". They are not proposed as a ruling on who may baptize.
