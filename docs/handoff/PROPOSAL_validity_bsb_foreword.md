## What the foreword says, in paraphrase

| Locator | Paraphrase |
|---|---|
| p. vii, lead-in to the propositions | Strict Old School Baptists claim to be of New Testament church origin and order, and they ground that claim in their doctrine of salvation and of baptism. |
| p. vii, prop. 1 | An assembly that preaches a false gospel, or practises a false baptism, or both, is not a true New Testament church of gospel order. |
| p. vii, prop. 2 | Profession precedes baptism. Believers are the only subjects, and immersion is the only mode. |
| p. vii, prop. 3 | Without Scriptural baptism there is no Scriptural church. |
| p. vii, prop. 6 | Mode, subject, purpose and administrator are fixed by Christ. |
| p. viii, prop. 7 | Only churches of New Testament origin and order can give Scriptural baptism. The proposition's own inference is that a society preaching a false gospel cannot give it. |
| p. viii, ramification 1 | Rome preaches a false gospel, so its baptism is invalid. Thornwell and the 1845 Old School General Assembly are cited as agreeing. |
| p. viii, ramification 2 | Whoever has only Roman baptism has no baptism. A denomination founded on Roman baptism therefore has no church validity, by prop. 3. Dabney is cited for the same conclusion. |
| p. viii, after the ramifications | This is the stated ground for receiving, by baptism, anyone who comes from a society not of like faith and order. All Protestant denominations are said to rest on Roman and infant baptism. |
| pp. ix–xiv | Sections II–V and the conclusion argue for knowing Baptist history and for republishing. **No succession chain is claimed anywhere**: none from John the Baptist, none through named churches, and no church identity. |

## Proposal: position 3, with one flag

**I propose (3): order defined by doctrine.** The reasons:

1. **Prop. 7's test is doctrinal.** The one inference prop. 7 draws is from a false gospel. No line of churches enters into it.
2. **Prop. 1 defines "gospel order" by gospel and baptism.** These are the same two marks as the plumb-line's statement for (3): justification, and right administration.
3. **No identity or succession claim appears in the whole foreword** (pp. vi–xiv). It does not require a church of *our* order by descent. Its rule for receiving members is likeness of faith and order, not membership in a line.

**The flag: the architect's "(4) in doctrinal dress" is partly right, and Chris should rule on it.**

- Prop. 3 says no baptism means no church. Prop. 7 says only a true church can baptize.
- Taken together, these make validity *recursive*: a church is valid only if its founders' baptism was valid, and that baptism was valid only if a valid church gave it.
- Ramification 2 applies the recursion historically. It does not only test Protestant bodies' present doctrine. It also disqualifies them by their *origin* in Roman baptism.
- Followed back, the recursion needs an unbroken chain of validly baptized founders. That is Graves's argument (Old Landmarkism, Ch. X: no unbaptized man can originate a church). The foreword never makes that argument, but its premises commit it to it.
- Chris's wording for (3) adds "not identity or succession". That holds only if the recursion is cut somewhere, for example by letting a church of right doctrine be gathered afresh.
- **So the line between (3) and (4) is one question: may a church of gospel order be gathered de novo?**
  - Graves says no. He holds that no unbaptized man ever had authority to originate baptism, or a church, de novo (Old Landmarkism, Ch. X, p. 124, checked on the 1880 scan). Yet Graves, like the foreword, recognises a true church by its marks of doctrine and order (Chs. II–VII). It is p. 124 that makes him (4) ([../sources/graves-old-landmarkism.md](../sources/graves-old-landmarkism.md)).
  - The foreword never answers this question.
  - Gill's *Divine Right*, Ch. II, pp. 9–13, may answer it the other way: John the Baptist administered baptism unbaptized. This is located from OCR only and is unchecked.

**How to model the flag, not hard-code it.**

- Prop. 3 (no baptism, no church) is a claim on the **church Locus**, which is phase 2 (§7's dependencies).
- When that Locus exists, the edge to look at is between "no baptism, no church" combined with (3) on one side, and (4) on the other. The question is whether the combination entails succession.
- The foreword sits on (3) until then.
